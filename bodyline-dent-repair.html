What It Costs

What You'll Pay - No Hidden Costs

Bodyline work is priced on its own merits, so there is no flat rate published here. You get a price from a photo before anything is booked.

A dent on a bodyline costs more than the same size dent on a flat panel, and it is worth knowing why. The line itself has to end up dead straight, because the eye follows it down the whole side of the car and picks up the smallest wave. That means more small pushes, more checking against the board, and more time.

The price is fixed before we start. If the line has been stretched or the metal is torn where it folded, PDR on its own will not give you a straight line back, and we will say so up front instead of getting halfway and stopping.
  • Send a photo along the lineStand at the end of the car and shoot down the length of the bodyline. That angle shows us how far the line has moved, which a straight on photo hides completely.
  • Priced for the crease, not the dentWe quote for putting the line back true, which is the actual job. Anyone pricing it as a plain dent is either not looking properly or not planning to finish it.

The Repair

What To Expect

Bodyline repairs are the ones that separate a decent PDR job from a rushed one. This is how ours goes.

  • The line is mapped firstThe reflection board goes on and we follow the line from both ends. Where the crease dips, kinks or runs wide shows up as a break in the reflection long before it shows to the naked eye.
  • High points come down before low points come upA folded bodyline almost always has metal sitting proud beside the dent. That gets tapped down first. Push the low spot up before dealing with it and the line ends up wavy.
  • The crease is rebuilt in small stepsWorking from the outside of the damage inwards, the metal is eased back until the line is continuous again. This is slow deliberate work and it is where most of the time on the job goes.
  • Checked from every angle you would look fromStraight on, down the length of the car, and in daylight rather than under a lamp. If the line reads true from all of them, the repair is done.

Expect a bodyline dent to take longer than a plain door ding. Half a day is common for a bad one, and that time is what buys you a line that still looks like the factory put it there.
